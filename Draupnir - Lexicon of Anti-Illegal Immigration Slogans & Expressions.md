# Lexicon of Anti-Illegal Immigration Slogans & Expressions

> Comprehensive English-language reference of slogans, catchphrases, hashtags, and short expressions used by those opposed to illegal immigration worldwide. Compiled for legal campaign use.

---

## 1. Core Principles — Short & Universal

These work across borders — they distill the argument to its simplest form.

| Slogan | Meaning / Context |
|--------|-------------------|
| **"Illegal is illegal."** | The tautological bedrock — no further justification needed. |
| **"What part of illegal don't you understand?"** | Confrontational, widely used on bumper stickers and signs in the US. |
| **"No one is above the law."** | Frames illegal entry as lawlessness, not misfortune. |
| **"A country without borders is not a country."** | Attributed to various sources; a sovereignty-first argument. |
| **"Secure borders, safe communities."** | The positive framing — border control as public safety. |
| **"Law and order starts at the border."** | Links immigration enforcement to the broader rule of law. |
| **"Sovereignty is non-negotiable."** | Academic register — used in EU political discourse. |
| **"My country, my rules."** | Populist, direct, universal. |
| **"Respect the queue."** | UK/Australian flavor — frames legal migrants as the ones being disrespected. |
| **"We have nothing against immigrants — we have a problem with illegal aliens."** | Distinction between legal and illegal migration. |

| Slogan | Notes |

| **"Laws mean nothing if they're not enforced."** | Universal legal principle. |
| **"Citizenship matters."** | Pushback against post-national / open-borders ideology. |
| **"Sovereignty begins at the border."** | Academic/think-tank register. |

## 6. Cultural & Demographic

The most rhetorically charged category — handle with precision in campaigns.

| Slogan | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| **"Preserve our culture."** | Universal, used from Europe to Japan. |
| **"Integration, not replacement."** | Nods to integration as the goal while opposing demographic change. |
| **"You cannot import a new population overnight and expect harmony."** | Pragmatic, not ideological. |
| **"Diversity requires borders."** | Counter-narrative: true diversity of nations requires separation. |
| **"A nation is more than an economy — it is a people."** | Communitarian. |
| **"Assimilation, not colonization."** | Sharp distinction — immigrants must adopt host culture. |
| **"Our heritage is not hate."** | Preemptive defense against accusations of racism. |
| **"Love your own people."** | Universalist-flipped — if all peoples are equal, loving your own is legitimate. |
| **"There is nothing wrong with wanting to live with your own people."** | Descriptive/pragmatic. |
| **"Mass immigration is not inevitable — it is a policy choice."** | Agency framing. |
| **"No one voted for this."** | Democratic-deficit argument — demographic change without consent. |
| **"Tradition is not extremism."** | Defensive. |
| **"Every people has the right to exist as a people."** | Universalizes the claim. |
| **"Cultural enrichment or cultural replacement?"** | Question framing — lets the listener answer. |

| **"Environmental sustainability means population stability."** | Green-conservationist angle — rare but present. |
| **"Human trafficking thrives on open borders."** | Flipping the humanitarian argument: loose borders = trafficker opportunity. |
| **"Real compassion is legal immigration with integration support."** | Positive alternative. |
| **"Stop the boats to stop the deaths."** | Australian/Mediterranean framing — tough policy as humanitarian. |

## 13. Policy-Shorthand Terms (Code Words & Acronyms)

These aren't slogans per se, but they function as in-group signals.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Chain migration** | Family-based migration beyond nuclear family. |
| **Anchor baby** | US — child born to illegal aliens on US soil, conferring birthright citizenship. |
| **Birth tourism** | Traveling to give birth on foreign soil for citizenship. |
| **Catch and release** | Releasing border-crossers pending court dates. |
| **Dreamers** | DACA recipients (pro-legalization term; anti-illegal-immigration side uses it cautiously). |
| **DACA** | Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals — policy, not slogan. |
| **E-Verify** | Electronic employment verification. |
| **ICE** | Immigration and Customs Enforcement (US). |
| **MPP / Remain in Mexico** | Migrant Protection Protocols. |
| **Visa overstayer** | Person who entered legally but stayed past visa expiry — the real majority of illegal immigrants in the US. |
| **Sanctuary city / state** | Jurisdiction that limits cooperation with federal immigration enforcement. |
| **Operation Sovereign Borders** | Australian policy brand name. |
| **Rwanda Plan** | UK policy for offshore processing. |
| **Pushback** | Interception and return at border — controversial under international law. |
| **Safe third country** | Legal doctrine — asylum must be claimed in the first safe country reached. |
| **Pull factor** | Policies or conditions that attract migration — e.g., welfare, sanctuary, weak enforcement. |
| **Brain drain** | Mass emigration of skilled workers from developing countries. |
| **Remittance drain** | Economic argument — money sent home doesn't circulate locally. |
| **Parallel society** | Immigrant communities that don't integrate — term used across Europe. |
| **No-go zones** | Contested term for areas where state authority is weak. |
| **Jus soli / Jus sanguinis** | Birthright citizenship (soil) vs. blood-descent citizenship. |
| **Economic migrant vs. refugee** | Distinction between those fleeing persecution and those seeking better economic prospects. |
| **Bogus asylum seeker** | Person abusing the asylum system for economic migration. |
| **Queue jumper** | UK/AUS — someone who enters illegally ahead of legal applicants. |
| **People smuggler** | Criminal facilitating illegal border crossings (distinct from "human trafficker" — smuggling is consensual transport, trafficking is exploitation). |
| **Regularization** | EU term for amnesty / legalizing status of illegal residents. |
| **Secondary movement** | EU — asylum seekers moving from first safe country to preferred destination. |
| **Dublin Regulation** | EU — asylum claim must be processed in first EU country of entry. |

## 16. Long-Form Statements & Mission-Style Language

## 19. Usage Notes for Campaign Design

### Tonal Spectrum

| Tone | Audience | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Legalist** | Moderates, professionals, center-left | "Rule of law starts at the border." |
| **Populist** | Working-class, disaffected voters | "My country, my rules." |
| **Patriotic** | Conservatives, nationalists | "Strong borders, strong nation." |
| **Economic** | Labor, unions, working-class left | "Protect our workers." |
| **Humanitarian-flipped** | Liberals, progressives | "Real compassion is legal, orderly immigration." |
| **Confrontational** | Base energizing | "What part of illegal don't you understand?" |

### Messaging Principles

1. **Lead with legality, not identity.** Law-based arguments are universal. Identity-based arguments divide your coalition.
2. **Distinguish, distinguish, distinguish.** Always separate legal from illegal, immigrant from alien, refugee from economic migrant. Conflation is the enemy of persuasion.
3. **Offer solutions, not just opposition.** "Enforce the law" implies a fix. "They're ruining the country" is just complaint.
4. **Use the opponent's values against them.** "You support the minimum wage? Then you must support border control — or the minimum wage becomes meaningless."
5. **Compassion is not weakness.** "We don't blame the migrant. We blame the smuggler and the politician." This disarms the "you're heartless" attack.
6. **Avoid radioactive terminology.** "Great replacement," "remigration," "invasion," and "14 words" are terminally toxic outside the hardest base. They don't persuade — they repel.
7. **Specific beats general.** "Stop the boats" beats "control immigration." Concrete and visual beats abstract.
8. **Positive beats negative.** "Secure borders, safe communities" beats "Stop the invasion." One builds, one tears down.
